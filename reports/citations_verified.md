## Result in one paragraph

Every source in the core set and the second tier was located, and its title, authors and year were confirmed. Each is classified below by how strongly its venue is confirmed. 31 have a venue stated on a publisher, proceedings, archive or conference page, or in the paper's own footer or header in an author- or archive-hosted copy. 6 rest on a publisher-deposited DOI record (Crossref) plus Semantic Scholar, because the ACM Digital Library returned 403, dblp showed a bot challenge, and the IEEE and Elsevier pages could not be read; for some of them an author or repository page adds support. 2 have a venue stated only in the arXiv comments, because the proceedings do not exist yet. 5 are arXiv-only with no venue found. 4 are partly verified: two are products and not papers, one has a paywalled full text, and one has its venue under a different title. The section "Corrections to the prior-art report" lists what the report got wrong or left open.

## Method and limits

Evidence was read directly: arXiv abstract pages (title, authors, comments, versions) and HTML full texts; ACL Anthology, CVF open access, ICLR and NeurIPS proceedings, PMLR, Springer, VLDB and NeurIPS event pages; the Crossref API for DOI records; Semantic Scholar's API as a cross-check; PyPI's JSON for VectorVFS; and author- or archive-hosted PDFs (CMU, UMass CIIR, Columbia, MIT, Ghent, the authors' own pages, a SIGIR Forum reprint) for the papers' own footers. A web search was used only to find URLs, never as evidence. Titles, author lists, page ranges, volumes and years in the `.bib` were re-checked by script against the saved sources and fresh Crossref records (every entry except the two non-paper sources), every footer or phrase quoted from a PDF was machine-checked against the downloaded text, and the `.bib` was test-compiled with BibTeX.

What could not be read, and was not worked around: the ACM Digital Library (403), dblp (bot challenge, and WebFetch is barred by its robots.txt), OpenReview (browser check), IEEE Xplore (empty response), GitHub's own pages and API (the session has no GitHub access), and the Internet Archive (403). Semantic Scholar rate-limited some requests and answered others. Venues at ICLR and NeurIPS are therefore confirmed from their proceedings sites, not from OpenReview. WebFetch summaries were used in three places only (the VectorVFS releases page, the RAPTOR lookalike PDF title, and an early NeurIPS cross-check) and each is marked or backed by a raw page.
