# dirvec session 4: cost axis

Question: does the container layer (a, b, c) buy anything over the flat walk d once cost counts?
Encoder, revision, input rules and ground truth as in session 3, except the .xls rule below.
Hardware: NVIDIA RTX PRO 4500 Blackwell (32 GB) for embedding; retrieval and timing on CPU
(numpy 2.5.2, one BLAS thread). Both corpora and both caches were rebuilt from scratch on a new pod.

## Summary

1. The .xls rule change moves session 3 numbers by at most 0.010 in any cell. H still survives:
   c - a is +0.188 [+0.124, +0.259] in cell 1 and +0.197 [+0.133, +0.268] in cell 2.
2. Two-stage retrieval with c and k = 10 comes within 0.002 of the flat walk on file recall on
   both sets (hit@1 -0.001 and -0.000, hit@10 -0.002 and -0.001) while scoring 48 to 49 percent of
   the vectors and taking about 30 percent of the query time. It is a small loss, not a tie: the
   session 1 hit@10 interval [-0.005, -0.001] excludes zero; the other three contain or touch it.
3. It never beats the flat walk. No two-stage setting has a hit@1 or hit@10 above d with an
   interval that excludes zero in the all-queries cell of either set. The container layer buys
   speed, not recall.
4. The pooled centroid a is the cheapest (11 to 12 percent of the vectors, 11 percent of the time at
   k = 10) and pays for it: hit@1 is 0.022 to 0.033 below d over all queries and 0.09 to 0.14 below
   d in the minority-modality cells, where k = 10 does not close the gap.
5. b sits between: at k = 10, hit@1 is 0.007 to 0.009 below d with 25 percent of the vectors.
6. Storage goes the other way. A two-stage index keeps the file vectors for stage 2 and adds the
   representatives, so it is larger than the flat index: a +8 percent, b +21 percent, c +44 to 45 percent.
7. Scale caveat: 400 directories and about 4,800 files. At this size every method answers in under
   1 ms and Python overhead is a visible share of the two-stage times. The ratios say little about
   a tree of millions of files, where stage 1 would itself need an index.

## Steps 2 and 3: method

Script: `scripts/twostage.py` (definitions in its docstring, fixed before the first run on real
vectors). Query and leave-one-out as in `eval.py`. Stage 1 ranks the 400 directories with a, b
or c and keeps the top k; stage 2 ranks the embedded files of those directories by cosine. The
flat walk d ranks every embedded file. The relevant files of a query are its embedded siblings.
- dir recall@k: the query's directory is among the k kept. Equal to `eval.py` recall@k.
- hit@n: at least one sibling among the top n files.
- sib@10: siblings among the top 10 files, divided by min(10, number of siblings).
  Read it with care at small k: with few directories kept, the candidate list is short and mostly
  siblings whenever stage 1 is right, so sib@10 rises as k falls. It measures how clean the list
  is, not how often the directory is found.
- vectors scored: representatives of all directories plus the files of the k kept directories,
  mean per query.
- Differences are two-stage minus flat, with paired 95% bootstrap intervals over directories
  (1000 resamples).
- Query time: one query at a time, brute force, full representations, top 10 files returned,
  best of 5 passes.
One query per set has no embedded sibling and is dropped from the file-level numbers.
Session 1 numbers use the ground truth as replaced in session 3 (4,111 queries, 4,067 with a vector).
