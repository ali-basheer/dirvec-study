# dirvec session 6: a representative budget

Question: does a representative budget that scales with directory size keep c's recall at a real
saving on the session 5 set?
Same set, cache, encoder, input rules and ground truth as session 5 (`results/session5.md`):
2,808 directories, 27,243 embedded files, 23,119 queries with at least one embedded sibling.
No download, no GPU. Retrieval and timing on CPU (MIG 1g.24gb pod, 3.4 vCPUs by cgroup;
numpy 2.5.2, scikit-learn 1.9.1, hnswlib 0.8.0), `DIRVEC_THREADS`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS` and
`OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS` set to 3, one BLAS thread for query times. The three runs went one after
another, each alone on the CPU (eval 5 min, twostage 15 min, hnsw 8 min).

New representations (`scripts/eval.py` `build()`, stage 1 lists of `twostage.py` and `hnsw.py`):
- c4: per-modality k-means with k = min(3, ceil(n_m / 4)) for a modality group of n_m embedded
  files; unit-normalised centroids, otherwise as c (n_init 10, seed 0; a group with k = n_m keeps
  its file vectors).
- c2: as c with k = min(2, n_m).

## Hypothesis (copied verbatim from BRIEF.md, fixed before the first eval run)

H6: on the session 5 set, c4, per-modality k-means with k = min(3, ceil(n_m / 4)) representatives
for a modality group of n_m embedded files (unit-normalised centroids, otherwise as c), used as
stage 1 of two-stage retrieval at k = 10, loses no more than 0.01 hit@10 against the exact flat walk
d over all queries and scores at most 0.35 of the vectors.
Kill: H6 is dead if the paired 95% bootstrap interval (1000 resamples over directories) of hit@10,
c4 at k = 10 minus exact d, over all queries lies entirely below -0.01, or if the mean share of
vectors scored per query by c4 at k = 10 is above 0.35. Report it plainly either way.
Secondary, reported, no kill: c2 (k = min(2, n_m)) the same way; c4 and c2 in the session 3 primary
cells (recall@5, c4 - a and c2 - a); c4 and c2 with HNSW over the representatives as stage 1,
against flat HNSW at the nearest query time.

## Verdict

H6 is dead. It fails the cost clause of its kill criterion: the mean share of vectors scored per
query by c4 at k = 10 is 0.355 (9,665 of 27,242 per query on average), above 0.35.
The recall clause holds: over all 23,119 queries, hit@10 of c4 at k = 10 minus exact d is -0.007,
95% CI [-0.009, -0.004], which does not lie entirely below -0.01.
The miss is narrow and it is stage 1 that carries it: c4 keeps 9,512 representatives, 0.349 of the
file vectors, before stage 2 adds about 150 files from the top 10 directories. The threshold was
fixed before the run and is not moved here.

## Reproduction check (before any c4 or c2 number was read)

With c4 and c2 added, `ranks.jsonl` (eval.py) and `twostage.jsonl` (twostage.py) for a, b, c and d
are identical, query by query, to the session 5 files (23,130 rows each; every rank and every
metric of every k). All a, b, c and d rows of the eval and twostage tables below equal
`results/session5.md`. Query times differ by a few percent between runs, as expected.
The HNSW numbers are not bit-identical: the index is built on 3 threads and insertion order
across threads is not fixed. Flat HNSW hit@10 moves by up to 0.002 (ef_search 16: -0.018 here,
-0.016 in session 5; ef_search 128: -0.003 here, -0.004 in session 5); the recall columns of the
two-stage HNSW rows for a, b and c are unchanged to 0.001.

1. c4 cuts stage 1 from 5.27 to 3.39 representatives per directory and the share of vectors
   scored from 0.549 to 0.355, for 0.006 hit@10 against c (0.797 to 0.791) and 0.003 hit@1. Its
   directory recall@10 is the same as c's (0.813). Its share is 0.005 above the pre-registered
   limit.
2. c2 (at most 2 per modality) scores 0.433 of the vectors and loses 0.006 hit@10 against d,
   [-0.008, -0.003]: about the same recall as c4 at a larger stage 1. Under the H6 rule c2 would
   pass on recall and fail on cost. Scaling the budget with group size is the better lever of the
   two: c4 is cheaper than c2 at equal recall.
3. Where it costs: c4's loss is concentrated in the minority-modality cells. At k = 10, hit@10
   minus d is -0.021 [-0.029, -0.013] for P1 (image queries in [0,.2)+[.2,.5)) and -0.020
   [-0.027, -0.012] for P2 (text-like queries in [.5,.8)+[.8,1]), against -0.009 and -0.007 for c.
   Likely cause, not tested: a minority group is small, so ceil(n_m / 4) gives it one centroid
   where c kept up to 3 (all its files when n_m <= 3). In the majority cells c4 is
   within 0.006 of d (image in [.5,.8)+[.8,1]: +0.004 [-0.001, +0.011]).
4. Session 3 primary cells, recall@5 (eval.py, leave-one-out directory recall): c4 - a is +0.230
   [+0.199, +0.259] (P1) and +0.217 [+0.190, +0.246] (P2); c2 - a is +0.231 [+0.199, +0.260] and
   +0.226 [+0.200, +0.254]. Both exclude zero in both cells, as c does (+0.238, +0.233). c4 is
   0.008 and 0.016 below c there; c2 0.007 and 0.007 below.
5. HNSW stage 1 (M = 16, ef_construction = 200, 100 representatives, exact stage 2 over 10
   directories) keeps the exact two-stage recall for both (directory overlap 0.999 to 1.000):
   c4 at ef_search 100 is 334 us, hit@10 -0.007 [-0.009, -0.004]; c2 is 339 us, -0.006
   [-0.008, -0.003]. The flat HNSW setting nearest in time is ef_search 128 (398 us), at -0.003
   [-0.004, -0.003]; ef_search 64 (229 us) is at -0.006 [-0.007, -0.005]. Flat HNSW is faster than
   HNSW c4 for the same loss (ef 64), or loses less at a similar time (ef 128). With HNSW c
   (365 us, -0.001) the container layer at least bought a few thousandths of hit@10; with the
   smaller budget it buys nothing against flat HNSW.
6. Storage: HNSW c4 needs its 79.3 MB index plus 223.2 MB of file vectors, 302.5 MB, 1.33 times
   the flat HNSW (227.2 MB); c2 320.2 MB, 1.41 times; c 346.7 MB, 1.53 times.
7. Brute force query time at k = 10 follows the share: c4 1,860 us, c2 2,251 us, c 2,787 us,
   flat d 5,088 us (0.37, 0.44, 0.55 of flat).

Against exact flat search, c4 is a real saving (about a third of the scoring for -0.007 hit@10),
just not the one H6 promised. Against an approximate flat index it is not a saving at all.
